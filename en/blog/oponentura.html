Opponent review before sign-off

The strongest case against your position, with a verdict per point — before the final output.

Colleagues keep saying the same thing: they would welcome a well-founded opponent review before the final output goes out. Not “what is wrong with the document” — that is review — but does our position survive?

It runs read-only and ideally in fresh context, so the critic is not the author. It steelmans the counter-case and attacks the load-bearing evidence and reasoning.

A verdict, not an impression

  • confirmed — the position held.
  • revised — it held after adjustment.
  • rejected — it did not hold.
  • contested — both sides are honest; for each we state what would change the verdict.

An opponent review that finds nothing must show the attempts. Rejected and dismissed objections are recorded too — otherwise it is theatre, not a check.

For high-risk matters, opponent review is always proposed before the final output.
